# Columns of Tabs, not one thing per Column

The reading workspace is a row of Columns. The Reading Pane is the first and cannot be closed; beside it sit Columns the reader creates, and each of those holds one or more Tabs with one showing at a time. A workspace-level `+` makes a new Column, a `+` in a Column's own strip adds a Tab to it, and both open the same flat menu of everything openable — the three translations, the three tafsirs, and AI — so no menu ever leads to another menu. Exactly one menu is open at a time across the whole workspace, which is why that state lives in the Tabs provider rather than in each `<details>` element as it did when there were three fixed buttons.

The obvious-looking design is one thing per Column, and the reason it is not that is worth recording: Tabs within a Column are alternatives the reader flicks between, Columns are what they see at once. A reader comparing three translations wants them stacked in one Column and a tafsir permanently visible beside it — a flat one-per-Column model can express the second half and not the first, and would spend a Column, on a layout where width is the scarce resource, on each translation they only ever look at one of.

Width is what sets the rest of the shape. Columns are capped at three at `xl` and four at `2xl`; past that the Arabic at its reading size stops being readable. At the cap the workspace `+` is disabled with a reason rather than hidden, and the per-Column `+` keeps working — the ceiling pushes the reader into the Tab mechanism rather than merely refusing them. A Column closes with its last Tab, because an empty one costs width and buys nothing. Order is fixed and widths are equal: neither drag-to-reorder nor drag-to-resize is implied by anything the workspace has to do, both carry real work in keyboard-accessible equivalents and persisted proportions, and at a cap of two or three reader Columns, closing and reopening one is a two-click reorder.

Below `xl` nothing can sit beside anything, so a narrow screen flattens every Tab from every Column into the single swipeable strip it already had, and `+` appends to it. The grouping is hidden at that width, not discarded — a reader who arranges Columns on a laptop and opens the same session on a phone has not lost the arrangement.

The arrangement is persisted to the reader's browser, which is what makes this expensive to reverse and is the reason for this record. Reading Position is kept for the same reason — the app should remember where the reader was without being asked — and a Column layout takes more effort to build than the single Tab it replaces. What is stored stays inside the boundary ADR 0001 draws: language and source codes, and for an AI Tab its Ayah reference, selection offsets and translation language, from which the texts are re-derived out of the corpus on restore. Conversations come back grounded but empty. Restoring a chat transcript out of `localStorage` is a materially different privacy claim from restoring which panels were open, and it is not one this app makes.
