# Static lexical retrieval index

RAG grounding retrieves over a BM25 index of the vendored translations and tafsir, built at build time by `scripts/build-retrieval-index.mjs` and shipped as one static JSON shard per reader language. Chosen over dense embeddings because build-time and query-time vectors must come from the same model, and this app has neither a backend nor a fixed provider: readers bring their own (ADR 0001), Groq exposes no embeddings endpoint at all, and an Ollama reader would need one exact model pulled or retrieval silently mismatches. Shipping a sentence-transformer to the browser would fix the model but costs a ~25 MB download and a heavy dependency, and a float32 index over the ~31k-passage corpus is larger than the 7.4 MB corpus itself.

Terms occurring in more than 5% of a language's passages are dropped. This is mostly a size cut — the commonest terms have the longest posting lists — but it also buys quality outright: a question is largely the words it is wrapped in ("what does the Quran say about…"), and enough of those together outweigh the one word the question is really about. Measured over the built corpus, dropping at a twentieth is what turns "patience in hardship" from commentary that merely says "hardship" into 2:45 and 94:5-6.

Retrieval is reached through its own seam, `CorpusRetriever`, rather than as a method on the Quran Content Repository as the MVP spec (#1) sketched it. The repository answers "what does the corpus say at this reference?"; retrieval asks "where in the corpus does this question point?" — the opposite direction, and the retriever is a consumer of the repository rather than a peer of its methods. Keeping them apart is what lets the AI Client be tested against a retriever with no index and the retriever be tested against no corpus. Trade-off: the AI Client now takes two injected dependencies rather than one.

Trade-off: retrieval is by shared wording, not by meaning, so a paraphrase sharing no words with the corpus finds nothing. This lands hardest on Turkish, where agglutination spells one word many ways, and on German, whose compound nouns never split — and German has no tafsir edition, so its shard indexes half the passages the others do. The AI Client tells the model how the passages were found so it can pass over the ones that missed, and retrieval is treated throughout as what makes an answer better grounded, never what makes one possible: every failure path ends in fewer passages rather than no answer.
