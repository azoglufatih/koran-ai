# A marked span of tafsir is a commentator's claim, never the Ayah's words

A reader can select in a Tafsir Tab and ask about what they selected, the way they already can in the Arabic, a Translation Tab and the Transliteration. That adds a fourth `AyahTextRole` — but not a fourth text of the same kind. The other three are the same Ayah rendered three ways: its script, its meaning, its sound. Tafsir is somebody's explanation *of* the Ayah. So a Commentary Selection is carried to the model attributed to the edition it came from and prompted as a claim about the Ayah — "the reader is asking about this sentence of Al-Mukhtasar's commentary on 2:40" — and the Grounding Notice shows it the same way: the source named, and none of the styling that marks Quran text.

The obvious implementation is the one this rejects. Every part of the machinery invites treating tafsir as a fourth text exactly like the other three — `AYAH_TEXT_ROLES` is a flat list, `AyahTextList` renders translation and tafsir through the same component, and the marking scheme would work unchanged. Taking that path would put a commentator's sentence into the prompt in the same voice as revelation, and a model given the two indistinguishably will answer about the gloss as though it were the text. For a reader who cannot read the Arabic — the reader this whole app is for — nothing downstream would reveal the substitution. That is the failure this exists to prevent, and it is worse than not offering the feature.

The cost is that the tafsir role is the one selectable text whose marked span cannot be read back out of the corpus by offset alone: it needs the source and the language as well, and its offsets mean nothing in another edition. A restored conversation therefore drops a Commentary Selection it cannot name the edition for, rather than restoring it against whichever tafsir the reader has open now.
