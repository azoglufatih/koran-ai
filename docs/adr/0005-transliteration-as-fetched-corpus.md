# Transliteration ships as fetched corpus, not bundled shell

The Latin-script Transliteration shown under each Ayah is vendored from `fawazahmed0/quran-api` — the same public-domain source as the translations — and served per Surah from `/content/transliteration/<scheme>/<surah>.json`, fetched when the Reading Pane renders rather than compiled into the pages the way the Arabic is. Three schemes ship, because the reader chooses which one they read: `ara-quranphoneticst` (the default, `Al-Ĥamdu Lillāhi Rabbi Al-`Ālamīna`), `ara-quran-la1` (plain ASCII, `Alhamdu lillaahi Rabbil 'aalameen`), and `tur-latinalphabet` (Turkish orthography, `El hamdü lillahi rabbil alemin`). A fourth upstream edition, `ara-quran-la`, is deliberately not offered: it encodes ayn as `AA`, which is a machine artifact a reader sounding the word out would read as "ay-ay".

Three schemes at ~1.2 MB each is the immediate reason they are fetched — the reader picks one and it is the only one that should ever cross the network. But the decision cuts against ADR 0004 in a way worth stating outright, because that ADR's reasoning does not anticipate this feature. The Arabic is bundled, so it survives in the App Shell cache and a reader who has opened a Surah can open it again with no network and see the text. The Transliteration will not be there. The effect is that offline reading works for everyone except the reader this feature exists for: someone who cannot read the script gets a page of Arabic they cannot sound out, which is worse than the translation being missing. That is a real cost, accepted because offline reading was scoped out of the MVP (#1) and remains undesigned — spending ~1.2 MB of every reader's first load, including the readers who turn the line off, to serve a case nobody has specified is paying now for a feature that does not exist. If offline reading is ever built, this is the first thing it should cover, ahead of the translations.

Two consequences follow from fetching rather than bundling. The Arabic is prerendered and the Transliteration is not, so a Surah reflows as the lines arrive; the Arabic is never held back waiting for them, because its being instant is the reason it is bundled at all. And the opening basmala of 112 Surahs sits outside the numbered Ayahs, where the vendored data — 6236 numbered Ayahs, nothing else — has no entry for it; it is rendered from the chosen scheme's own 1:1, which is that same text, so no scheme needs a hardcoded Latin string.

Retrieval does not index the Transliteration and the AI is not sent it, with one exception. It is phonetic rather than meaning-bearing, so indexing it could only match a question that happened to share Latin spellings, and ADR 0003 already drops the commonest terms because near-universal wording degrades results. The exception is a reader selecting *in* the Transliteration — "what does `Ar-Raĥmāni` mean?" — which is plausibly the most likely question this feature creates, and which nothing else on the page lets them ask. That adds a third `AyahTextRole` and sends the Transliteration marked alongside the unmarked Arabic. The app cannot itself map the selection onto Arabic characters, because the vendored data carries no word-level alignment; it hands the model both texts and relies on it to align them. That reliance is the trade-off, and it is confined to the one case where refusing it would leave the reader with no way to ask at all.
